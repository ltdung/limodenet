LIMODENet: restoring channel-degraded satellite imagery under a spiking constraint

Paper (arXiv) Code (GitHub) Weights (HuggingFace) Spiking dynamics page

Satellite images sent over a low-SNR DVB-S2X link at 1 dB arrive badly degraded. Every restorer here has about 0.75–0.78M parameters and was trained under the same protocol on the same data. Use this page to check two claims from the paper:

  1. LIMODENet restores better than the CNN-AE and the U-Net at matched parameter count. NAFNet-lite and Restormer-lite restore better still. They are included so you can see that.
  2. Only LIMODENet converts to a spiking network with zero blocked operations. NAFNet-lite and Restormer-lite each have more than 20.

Everything runs in your browser (ONNX Runtime Web, on the CPU). Each model is about 3 MB and is downloaded from the Hub the first time you use it. Numbers come from one seed (seed 0) on the 30 shipped images; the paper reports three seeds on the full test split, so expect small differences.

Input

Real 1 dB/q100 DVB-S2X test images, three per class, drawn with a fixed seed (not hand-picked).

Parameters, mult-adds and latency

Parameters and mult-adds (GMac, at 128×128) were counted from the PyTorch models with torch.utils.flop_counter, attention matmuls included. Latency is measured now, in your browser (ONNX Runtime Web, CPU, batch 1, median of 5 runs). The spiking model's numbers are T=8 dense passes on conventional hardware; this is not a neuromorphic-hardware measurement.
